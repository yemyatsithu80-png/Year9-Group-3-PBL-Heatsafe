What Heat Waves
Can Do

Heat waves affect our health, environment, economy, and daily life. The cards below explain the most serious dangers — click each one to read more.

Dehydration & Heat Stroke

When the body loses more water than it takes in, dehydration sets in, causing thirst, tiredness, dizziness, headache, and dark urine. Without rest and fluids it can turn into heat exhaustion (heavy sweating, weakness, nausea, fast pulse) and then heat stroke, a life-threatening emergency where body temperature rises above 40°C. Signs of heat stroke include confusion, slurred speech, hot and flushed skin, and fainting. Elderly people, babies and young children, pregnant women, people with long-term illnesses, and outdoor workers are most at risk. If you suspect heat stroke, call emergency services right away and start cooling the person down.

Wildfires

Long periods of heat and dry weather turn forests, grasslands, and bushes into fuel. A single spark, from lightning, a cigarette, or a vehicle, can start a fire that spreads fast. Wildfires destroy homes, wildlife habitats, and farmland. Their smoke pollutes the air and causes breathing problems, even in cities far away, sometimes for weeks. Wildfires also release large amounts of carbon dioxide, which adds to climate change and can make future heat waves worse.

Crop Failure & Food Shortage

Extreme heat dries out the soil and stresses plants, causing them to wilt, stop growing, or produce little or no fruit. A heat wave during flowering or grain-filling can cut yields sharply. This leads to higher food prices, shortages, and financial hardship for farmers. Heat also harms livestock, lowering milk and meat production. As heat waves become more frequent, protecting food supplies is a growing concern for many countries.

Power Outages

During a heat wave, demand for electricity jumps as people switch on fans, air conditioners, and refrigerators. Power grids can become overloaded, and extreme heat can also make power lines sag and transformers fail. The result can be blackouts that leave homes without cooling exactly when it is needed most, which is especially dangerous for the elderly and the sick. In some places, power companies use rolling blackouts to avoid a total grid failure.

Harm To Animals & Wildlife

Heat waves can kill birds, fish, and land animals. Rivers, ponds, and lakes can shrink or dry up, leaving fish and other water life without oxygen or space. Birds and mammals can suffer from heat exhaustion, and many lose their food sources when plants and insects die off. Marine heat waves can bleach coral reefs and damage whole underwater ecosystems. Wild animals that cannot move to cooler places or find shade may die in large numbers. Pets and farm animals are also at risk and need shade, fresh water, and cool places to rest.

Economic & Mental Impact

Heat waves cost billions of dollars every year. Workers become less productive, some businesses close, crops fail, and health care costs rise. Power shortages can stop factories and offices. Heat also affects the mind: it can cause stress, anxiety, irritability, and poor sleep, and some studies link very hot weather to more hospital visits for mental health problems. Extreme heat affects not just the body but also the mind and society as a whole.
