What Is A Heat Wave?

A heat wave is a period of abnormally hot weather that lasts for several days or longer, often with temperatures much higher than the usual average for that region. Heat waves can be dangerous, especially when combined with high humidity, because they put stress on the human body and the environment.

Understanding Heat Waves

Definition

There is no single worldwide definition of a heat wave. Each country sets its own thresholds, but most define it as several consecutive days and nights when temperatures are far above what is normal for that place and time of year. Unlike a single hot day, a heat wave lasts long enough to affect human health, power supplies, and natural ecosystems. Hot nights are especially risky because the body gets no chance to recover. High humidity makes it worse, because sweat evaporates more slowly and the body cannot cool itself as well, so even healthy people can quickly feel exhausted and weak. This is why the "feels like" temperature (also called the heat index), which combines heat and humidity, is often a better guide to danger than the air temperature alone.

Causes

Heat waves are caused by several factors. Climate change, driven by greenhouse gas emissions, is making heat waves hotter and more frequent worldwide. High-pressure systems in the atmosphere can sit over a region for days, trapping warm air near the ground and bringing clear skies that heat the land even more. Cities often become hotter than surrounding areas due to the "urban heat island" effect, where concrete, asphalt, and buildings absorb and re-radiate heat. Deforestation and loss of green spaces remove natural cooling, making heat waves feel even worse in cities. At night, cities also release the heat they stored during the day, so people get less relief.

Where & When They Happen

Heat waves can occur almost anywhere in the world, during the hottest months of the year. They can be dry and desert-like, or hot and humid in tropical and coastal areas. In recent years, countries across Asia, Europe, North America, Africa, and Australia have experienced record-breaking heat waves. Cities with fewer trees and more concrete are at greater risk. In some regions, heat waves also bring drought, wildfires, and power shortages. As global temperatures continue to rise, scientists expect heat waves to become longer, hotter, and more frequent in many regions.
