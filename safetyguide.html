Heat Wave Safety Guide

Below, you can explore simple and easy guides on how to stay safe during a heat wave. You can learn what supplies to prepare, the steps to protect yourself and your family, how to cool your home without air conditioning, the warning signs of heat-related illness, and the common mistakes people often make during extreme heat.

What You Need

  • Plenty of drinking water
  • Light-colored, loose clothing
  • Hat and sunglasses
  • Sunscreen (SPF 30 or higher)
  • Electric fan or access to a cool room
  • Electrolyte drinks or ORS packets
  • First-aid kit with cooling towels

Safety Steps

  1. Drink water regularly, even if you don't feel thirsty.
  2. Stay indoors or in the shade during the hottest hours (about 11 AM – 4 PM).
  3. Wear light-colored, loose-fitting clothes.
  4. Take cool showers or use damp cloths to cool down.
  5. Avoid alcohol and heavy meals, and limit sugary drinks.
  6. Avoid strenuous outdoor exercise.
  7. Check on elderly neighbours, children, and pets.

Cooling Your Home

  • Close curtains and blinds during the day.
  • Open windows at night for cool air flow.
  • Use fans to circulate air — but not if indoor air is hotter than outside.
  • Hang wet towels near windows or fans for evaporative cooling.
  • Turn off heat-producing appliances (ovens, dryers, computers).
  • Visit public cooling centres (libraries, malls, community halls) if needed.

Warning Signs To Watch For

  • Heavy sweating and weakness
  • Dizziness, headache, or nausea
  • Muscle cramps
  • Rapid heartbeat and breathing
  • Confusion, slurred speech, or fainting (possible heat stroke)
  • Hot, dry skin with no sweating (severe heat stroke)
  • Heat stroke is an emergency: call your local emergency number, move the person to a cool place, and cool them with water or wet cloths. Do not give drinks to someone who is confused or unconscious.

Common Mistakes To Avoid

  • Never leave children or pets in a parked car.
  • Don't wait until you're thirsty to drink water.
  • Don't exercise outdoors in peak heat.
  • Don't drink alcohol to cool down — it dehydrates you.
  • Don't ignore warning signs of heat exhaustion.
  • Don't rely only on fans when indoor temperatures are above 35°C.

HELPING OTHERS

Heat waves affect everyone, but some people need extra help. During extreme heat, take time to check on the people around us — a simple call or visit can save a life. Learn how we can help different groups below.

HELPING THE ELDERLY

Elderly people are at higher risk because their bodies lose the ability to regulate temperature as they age. Check on them at least twice a day, make sure they have water nearby, and help them stay in cool rooms.

Who to help: Parents, grandparents, and neighbours living alone.

PROTECTING PETS

Pets can suffer heat stroke too.Provide plenty of fresh water, keep them indoors during peak hours, never leave them in cars, and walk dogs early morning or late evening when the ground is cooler.

Who to help: Dogs, cats, birds, and small animals.

SUPPORTING OUTDOOR WORKERS

Outdoor workers such as construction workers, farmers, and delivery riders are exposed to extreme heat all day.Encourage frequent water breaks, shaded rest areas, and light clothing.If possible, shift heavy work to early morning or evening.

Who to help: Farmers, construction workers, and delivery riders.
